How learning builds

The order of units across the years, as set out in the curriculum map.

  1. Year 7

    Earlier learning (from the map)

    • Half Terms 1–2: How can humans fulfil life’s purpose? · Why is compassion important in the practice of religion? · What is compassion? …
    • Half Terms 3–4: Who was Guru Nanak Dev Ji and what virtues did They embody and teach?
    • Half Terms 5–6: How do religious people communicate and connect with God or the divine?

  2. Year 8

    Earlier learning (from the map)

    • Half Terms 1–2: How do religious people signify major landmarks in their religions?
    • Half Terms 3–4: Why is selfless service important in the practice of religion?
    • Half Terms 5–6: How do religious people kindle their connection with God or the divine?

  3. Year 9

    • Half Terms 1–2: Christian Beliefs · Sikh Beliefs
    • Half Terms 3–4: Living the Religious Life · Living a Christian Life
    • Half Terms 5–6: Philosophy of Religion · Equality

  4. Year 10

    • Half Terms 1–2: Marriage and the Family
    • Half Terms 3–4: Matters of Life and Death
    • Half Terms 5–6: Revision, preparation for examinations

Year 6 to 7 transition

The knowledge and skills embedded at Key Stage 2 are developed throughout Key Stages 3 and 4. Cross-phase collaboration ensures that there are links through the curriculum content in 3 main areas:

Religious literacy – Key features of religious tradition, historical context, scripture, expression of faith, religious leaders

Philosophical and ethical thinking – secular viewpoints, arguments for and against the existence of God, the meaning and purpose of life, ethical issues and theories

Virtue and Character development – virtues in religious communities, religious diversity, social justice & equality, the importance of community.
